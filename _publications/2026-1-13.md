Sleep stage classification and early detection of sleep disorders are critical for managing neurodegenerative and cardiovascular health. However, existing models often struggle with the long-range dependencies of multimodal polysomnography (PSG) signals and the domain shift across different clinical centers. In this study, we propose **SleepMambaNet**, a novel multimodal deep learning framework based on Selective State Space Models (Mamba) designed for robust sleep analysis. 

Leveraging a multicenter dataset, SleepMambaNet integrates electroencephalogram (EEG), electrooculogram (EOG), and electromyogram (EMG) data through a hierarchical Mamba-based architecture to capture both local signal fluctuations and global sleep architecture transitions. Our framework introduces a cross-modal fusion module that dynamically weighs the importance of different physiological streams, enhancing feature representation for complex stages such as REM and N2. Furthermore, SleepMambaNet demonstrates superior performance in the early identification of sleep-related breathing disorders and insomnia compared to traditional CNN and Transformer-based models. Experimental results across multiple clinical cohorts validate the models generalizability and efficiency, offering a scalable solution for automated sleep monitoring and precision diagnostics in large-scale clinical settings.
