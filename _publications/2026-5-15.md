Drug resistance remains a major challenge in cancer treatment, significantly affecting therapeutic efficacy. Although non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) play critical roles in mediating drug resistance, few computational models can effectively predict drug resistance-associated ncRNAs. Existing methods often fail to capture the complex sequence patterns of ncRNAs and their intricate interrelationships, leading to suboptimal performance. To overcome these limitations, we propose MIFNDRA, a multimodal integrative framework that jointly models ncRNA and drug features to identify ncRNAs linked to drug resistance. MIFNDRA uses a pre-trained Graph Isomorphism Network (GIN) to extract drug structural features and a pre-trained SpliceRNA model to encode ncRNA sequences. It further integrates diverse similarity features for both drugs and ncRNAs and enhances representation through a novel ncRNA interaction network, which incorporates both direct and indirect relationships among different ncRNA types as a knowledge-enhancement strategy. Leveraging advanced graph learning techniques—including residual GraphSAGE and contrastive learning—the model improves the identification of resistance-associated ncRNAs. Additionally, we curated a new benchmark dataset pairing ncRNA sequences with drug SMILES strings and resistance annotations. Comprehensive experiments show that MIFNDRA achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance. Case studies on cisplatin and gemcitabine further demonstrate the model’s robustness and its potential to advance drug resistance research and drug development.
