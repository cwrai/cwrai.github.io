Modest cognitive gains from amyloid immunotherapies underscore the need to address early, modifiable drivers of Alzheimer’s disease (AD). We propose the Glial Circadian-Immunometabolic Gating Collapse (GCIG-C) hypothesis, which links AD pathogenesis to the failure of circadian-timed glial switches. Healthy microglia and astrocytes dynamically oscillate between a wake-phase surveillance state fueled by glycolysis and a sleep-phase restorative state mediated by oxidative phosphorylation, lysosomal degradation, and glymphatic clearance. Circadian disruption or aging locks glia into a chronic, pro-inflammatory, and metabolically rigid state, crippling Aβ/tau clearance while escalating synaptic vulnerability. Anchored by the BMAL1/CLOCK–TREM2–APOE axis and aggravated by peripheral inflammation, this gating collapse represents a critical pathological tipping point. We provide testable predictions and discuss interventions—spanning chronotherapeutics, metabolic modulators, and 40 Hz sensory entrainment—reframing AD intervention from single-protein targeting toward systemic chronobiological rescue.
